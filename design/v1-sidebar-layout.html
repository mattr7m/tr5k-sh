Backcountry Network Monitoring with LoRa

Feb 18, 2026 camping infra

Last fall I spent a week in the Boundary Waters with a couple of Heltec V3 boards, a small solar panel, and an unreasonable desire to know the temperature of the lake at all times. This is what I learned about running a LoRa mesh network when the nearest cell tower is 40 miles away.

The Hardware

The Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 (V3) hits a good balance between cost and capability. At roughly $18 per board you can afford to lose one to a curious raccoon. I flashed each with Meshtastic firmware and packed them in waterproof cases from the dollar store.

Note

If you're considering the TTGO T-Beam as an alternative, be aware that the GPS module draws significantly more power. For stationary nodes, the Heltec is the better choice.

Node Configuration

Each node was configured with a 60-second broadcast interval. The base station node sat under a tarp near camp, powered by a 6W panel feeding a small LiFePO4 cell:

# meshtastic config for base node
meshtastic --set lora.region US
meshtastic --set lora.modem_preset LONG_SLOW
meshtastic --ch-set name "bwca-mesh"
meshtastic --set position.gps_enabled false
meshtastic --set power.wait_bluetooth_secs 30

The remote nodes were placed at the portage landing and the far shore — roughly 1.2km line-of-sight across the lake. Signal strength held surprisingly well over water, averaging around -95 dBm.

What Worked

Honestly, most of it. The mesh stayed up for five days straight without intervention. Temperature readings from a DS18B20 probe came through reliably, and the solar panel kept the base station charged even on overcast days. The whole setup packed down into a single dry bag weighing under 2 lbs.

Next time I want to add a small e-ink display at the base station and experiment with longer-range links across portages. But that's a post for another day.
